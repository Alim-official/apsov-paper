## Abstract

The paper considers the degradation of the first wall of a fusion reactor under the combined action of helium fluxes, hydrogen isotopes, and fast neutrons. A multilayer protective coating architecture is proposed: tungsten substrate (heat removal), h-BN enriched with boron-10 (neutron absorption), and graphene (barrier for helium and hydrogen).

The modeling was performed by molecular dynamics with neural network potentials (NEP89) in the GPUMD package. Validation: tungsten lattice parameter deviation 0%, C–C bond 0.02%, B–N bond 0.06%. The three-layer system is stable at 1200 K. Adhesion energy of the W/h-BN interface is −16.48 eV.

A separate test revealed that NEP89 is unsuitable for describing He–W interaction: predicted insertion energy −41.7 eV versus DFT value +4…+6 eV. A specialized helium potential is required — this is the next stage of the study.
